Choreographic Coding Lab

A Motion Bank format.

Facilitation

The Choreographic Coding Lab is a traveling laboratory founded by Motion Bank. For a few days, creative coders, dancers, choreographers, and researchers work side by side, translating choreographic thinking into digital form and applying code to movement, scores, and data. The emphasis is on experiment, invention, and peer-to-peer exchange.

Since 2013 the format has moved between cities. I facilitated the last two editions in the UK, invited by Aoi Nakamura and Esteban Lecoq of A+E Lab, their art and technology hub in Chatham, Kent.

Royal Ballet and Opera

The Choreographic Coding Lab at the Royal Ballet and Opera took place May 30–June 7, 2026, as part of the RBO/SHIFT festival. The public sharing was on June 7 at PLAY SHIFT. Participants worked inside the house, across costumes, archives, and technical production. I facilitated it with Anton Koch (Motion Bank) and Daniel Bisig (Zurich University of the Arts). Aoi Nakamura and Esteban Lecoq of AΦE / A+E Lab hosted. The lab was commissioned by the Royal Ballet and Opera, with support from ElevenLabs.

A+E Lab, Chatham

The second International Choreographic Coding Lab at A+E Lab ran March 24–28, 2025, in the Historic Dockyard, Chatham, with a public sharing on March 28. This was the lab’s second edition in the UK, and its focus was artificial intelligence. I facilitated it with David Rittershaus (Motion Bank, Hochschule Mainz) and Daniel Bisig (Centre for Dance Research, Coventry University, Zurich University of the Arts, and Motion Bank). Aoi Nakamura and Esteban Lecoq hosted the week.

The gathering opened the evening before, on March 23, with XR Sunday Social at A+E Lab.
